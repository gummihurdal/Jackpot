Rejected by the safety gate

Method

The model is Hafnia: a very cyclical but very strong company whose price swings across a wide range. Buy near the bottom, wait. Version 2 limits this to low and mid caps and adds hard safety checks.

1. Universe

All stocks between USD 300m and USD 10bn market value, with at least USD 1m traded per day, on the primary exchanges of the countries covered. Cross-listings are merged. Mainland China A-shares are left out because they are hard to buy from Europe; Chinese companies listed in Hong Kong or the US are included.

2. Price pattern

52-week high at least twice the low, and price in the lowest quarter of that range. Pharma and biotech get a looser test (high 1.6 times the low, price in the lowest 40%) so that more of them are examined.

3. Safety gate

A company is listed only if it passes every stage. Failing one puts it on the Rejected tab with the reason.

  • Funding - free cash flow is positive, or cash covers at least 2 years at the current burn rate
  • Debt - more cash than debt, or net debt no more than 3.5 times operating profit (6 times for utilities and property)
  • Liquidity - current ratio of at least 0.8
  • Dilution record - share count has not grown more than 20% in 3 years, corrected for stock splits
  • SEC filings (US-listed) - rejected for a bankruptcy, delisting-notice, withdrawn-accounts or late-report filing in the past year; a cash-burning company is also rejected if it filed a prospectus or issued unregistered shares in the past 6 months
  • News - rejected for a distress headline in the past 90 days, or a capital-raise headline while burning cash

Banks and insurers are judged on profitability instead of the first three.

4. Scores (0-100)

  • Quality - profits now and forecast, forward P/E, return on equity, net and gross margin, sales growth, free cash flow, analyst rating and target
  • Safety - how comfortably the gate was passed. Buybacks score highest, a rising share count lowest
  • Setup - range width, closeness to the low, rebound history (how often the stock fell 40%+ in 10 years and came back), and whether the price is turning, basing or still falling
  • Potential (pharma only) - active Phase 3 and Phase 2 trials the company leads and how soon the next Phase 3 result is due (ClinicalTrials.gov), sales and gross margin, sales growth, analyst rating and target

5. Spring score

Quality 35%, Safety 35%, Setup 30%. For pharma: Potential 30%, Safety 30%, Quality 20%, Setup 20%. Minus 12 points if the stock has been below its old peak for 3+ years. Recent headlines move the score by up to 6 points either way (buyback, raised guidance, positive trial result; or cut guidance, trial setback, legal issue).

6. The Top 20 list

A second, much stricter pass over everything that passed the safety gate. A stock is listed only if all of this holds:

  • It trades on a market a European private investor can normally reach (US, Canada, UK, euro area, Switzerland, Nordics, Poland, Israel, Japan, Hong Kong, Singapore, Australia, Mexico)
  • At least 3 years of price history, or a newcomer (listed 9 months to 3 years) that is profitable with stable or rising profit. At most 5 newcomers are listed, each marked
  • +200% test - within the last 3 years it held a level at least 3 times today's price for 13 weeks or more. The distance is measured to that level, never to a one-day top. Very few stocks pass this together with the other tests, so stocks at +150% or more that pass everything else fill the list up to 20, shown separately
  • Earnings test - at that old level it traded at no more than 40 times its best year's profit, so the level was earned, not hype
  • Profit is not collapsing (latest quarter not more than 50% below a year ago, unless it has risen two quarters running), annual profit has not fallen two years running, and the company is profitable
  • No research veto: when the written research finds a cause the numbers cannot see yet, such as a new competitor or a ban, the stock is moved to the near misses with that reason
  • No capital-raise, legal, filing or trial-setback warning, and no pending securities class action
  • Not more than 4 names from one theme

Stocks far below an old level but quiet over the last 12 months are pulled in as well, so the list is not limited to recent crashes. Each card's file shows history, earnings by year, the real dividend record, management and owners, the spike check and the risks. "Why it fell" and the assessment are written research, added by hand and dated; a card without it says so. "+200%" is the distance back to a price the stock really held. It is not a forecast.

7. Sources and memory

  • Prices, fundamentals, share counts and company headlines: Yahoo Finance
  • Filings: SEC EDGAR
  • Trials: ClinicalTrials.gov
  • Market-wide feeds: Benzinga top stories and FDA press releases, collected every few hours
  • Every scan stores a snapshot of the whole universe and all scores in a local database (Parquet files read with DuckDB), which powers the score changes, the market backdrop and the Tracker's benchmark

What it cannot see

  • Why a stock fell. A failed trial, a lawsuit or a lost patent looks the same as a market panic in the numbers.
  • A planned share issue that has not happened yet. The gate measures the need for money and the past record, not management's intentions.
  • Trial quality. A Phase 3 count says the pipeline is late-stage, not that the drug works.
  • Fundamentals and analyst targets lag: after a sharp fall, targets are often stale and too high.
  • Stocks that are all one trade. Many names can be down for the same reason.
