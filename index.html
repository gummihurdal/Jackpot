Method

The model is Hafnia: a very cyclical but very strong company whose price swings across a wide range. Buy near the bottom, wait. Version 2 limits this to low and mid caps and adds hard safety checks.

1. Universe

All stocks between USD 300m and USD 10bn market value, with at least USD 1m traded per day, on the primary exchanges of the countries covered. Cross-listings are merged. Mainland China A-shares are left out because they are hard to buy from Europe; Chinese companies listed in Hong Kong or the US are included.

2. Price pattern

52-week high at least twice the low, and price in the lowest quarter of that range. Pharma and biotech get a looser test (high 1.6 times the low, price in the lowest 40%) so that more of them are examined.

3. Safety gate

A company is listed only if it passes all four. Failing one puts it on the Rejected tab.

  • Funding - free cash flow is positive, or cash covers at least 2 years at the current burn rate
  • Debt - more cash than debt, or net debt no more than 3.5 times operating profit (6 times for utilities and property)
  • Liquidity - current ratio of at least 0.8
  • Dilution record - share count has not grown more than 20% in 3 years

Banks and insurers are judged on profitability instead of the first three.

4. Scores (0-100)

  • Quality - profits now and forecast, forward P/E, return on equity, net and gross margin, sales growth, free cash flow, analyst rating and target
  • Safety - how comfortably the gate was passed. Buybacks score highest, a rising share count lowest
  • Setup - range width, closeness to the low, rebound history (how often the stock fell 40%+ in 10 years and came back), and whether the price is turning, basing or still falling
  • Potential (pharma only) - active Phase 3 and Phase 2 trials the company leads (ClinicalTrials.gov), sales and gross margin, sales growth, analyst rating and target

5. Spring score

Quality 35%, Safety 35%, Setup 30%. For pharma: Potential 30%, Safety 30%, Quality 20%, Setup 20%. Minus 12 points if the stock has been below its old peak for 3+ years.

What it cannot see

  • Why a stock fell. A failed trial, a lawsuit or a lost patent looks the same as a market panic in the numbers.
  • A planned share issue that has not happened yet. The gate measures the need for money and the past record, not management's intentions.
  • Trial quality. A Phase 3 count says the pipeline is late-stage, not that the drug works.
  • Fundamentals and analyst targets lag: after a sharp fall, targets are often stale and too high.
  • Stocks that are all one trade. Many names can be down for the same reason.
