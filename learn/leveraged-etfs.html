Leveraged ETFs, one by one.

What funds like TNA, SOXL and TQQQ are, how the daily reset works, and for each one: what it follows, an example, and the market conditions it suits.

The basics

A leveraged ETF is a fund you buy like a normal share. It multiplies the daily move of an index by 2 or 3.

Take TNA. It follows the Russell 2000, an index of about 2,000 small US companies, at 3x. If the Russell 2000 rises 1% today, TNA rises about 3%. If the index falls 2%, TNA falls about 6%.

Most of these funds come in pairs. The bull fund rises when the index rises. The bear fund does the opposite: it rises when the index falls. TNA is the bull fund on small caps and TZA is its bear twin.

You never borrow money yourself. The fund does the leverage inside, using contracts with banks. The most you can lose is what you put in.

The daily reset: the one thing to understand

The 3x holds for one day. Every evening the fund resets, and the next day starts fresh at 3x. Over several days the result is not simply 3x the index. It depends on the path the index took.

Leveraged ETF or futures?

Leveraged ETF (e.g. TNA)Futures (e.g. RTY, M2K)
Where it tradesLike a share, in a normal broker accountFutures exchange, needs a futures account
LeverageFixed 2x or 3x, reset every dayFrom margin, depends on your position size
DecayYes, in choppy marketsNone, tracks the index directly
HoursUS market hours, thin before and afterAlmost 24 hours, Sunday evening to Friday
Most you can loseWhat you put inMore than your margin
ExpiryNoneQuarterly, must be rolled

Six rules before trading one

  1. Know the index behind it. You are trading the index, three times over. If you have no view on the index, you have no trade.
  2. Cut your size to a third. 1,000 in a 3x fund swings about as much per day as 3,000 in the index.
  3. Decide the exit before the entry. Set a stop and a target. A 3x fund can drop 10% in a morning.
  4. Trade trends, skip chop. These funds work on days and weeks when the index moves clearly one way. In sideways markets they bleed.
  5. Think in days, not months. The longer you hold, the further the result drifts from 3x. If you have to hold a position for several weeks, a 2x fund or the plain index fund is the calmer tool.
  6. Check the volume. The big ones (TQQQ, SOXL, SPXL, TNA) trade easily. The small ones have wide gaps between buy and sell price, which costs money on every trade.
